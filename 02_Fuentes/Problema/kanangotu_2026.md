## Resumen (de la base de datos, no de la lectura)
Lead generation in B2B marketing has witnessed a paradigm shift from mass outreach with limited qualification criteria to data-driven approaches that identify high-intent prospects based on behavioral signals, engagement patterns, and intent data. This evolution has enhanced sales-marketing alignment, with marketing becoming a strategic function for attracting, nurturing, and qualifying leads before entering the pipeline. In this evolving landscape, predictive lead scoring has emerged as a critical mechanism for prioritizing prospects in complex, multi-stakeholder B2B environments, leveraging AI-based models that continuously improve predictions based on historical data and behavioral patterns-moving beyond the static limitations of manually assigned, rule-based scoring. However, despite increasing adoption by practitioners, academic literature on predictive lead scoring remains fragmented, largely B2C-oriented, and empirically limited.
&lt;div&gt;
 This systematic review aggregates peer-reviewed and practitioner-academic literature between 2018 and 2025 to examine the impact of predictive analytics, artificial intelligence, intent data, and account-based marketing frameworks on buyer identification, lead qualification, and pipeline performance in B2B sectors such as technology, manufacturing, SaaS, and professional services. The systematic search was carried out for five major academic databases for relevant studies meeting pre-specified inclusion criteria following a PRISMA-guided protocol.&amp;nbsp;
&lt;/div&gt;
&lt;div&gt;
 The review identifies five major thematic clusters: (1) predictive lead scoring models and algorithms, (2) buyer and account identification, (3) intent data and behavioural signals, (4) personalisation and demand generation, (5) sales-marketing alignment.&amp;nbsp;
&lt;/div&gt;
&lt;div&gt;
 Results confirm that machine learning approaches, especially logistic regression, random forests and gradient boosting, consistently outperform traditional rule-based scoring in conversion accuracy. Analytics culture, data quality and CRM governance emerge as critical organisational enablers of effective implementation. This review consolidates a fragmented evidence base and highlights the organisational and technical conditions necessary for effective predictive lead scoring in B2B contexts, offering direction for both future research and practice.&amp;nbsp;
&lt;/div&gt;
&lt;div&gt;
 In addition, the first author’s personal experience as a marketing practitioner at a B2B data engineering firm in India, where predictive analytics and intent-data tools were implemented in live campaign and pipeline contexts, further informs this review. This practitioner perspective is employed, with the requisite degree of caution, to demonstrate how the dynamics described in the literature is manifested in daily commercial operations and reveals implementation realities, particularly 3 those related to organisational trust in algorithmic outputs, that have not yet been thoroughly documented in secondary academic reviews. There are substantial research voids that persist in the areas of SME contexts, emerging economies, longitudinal model performance, explainable AI adoption, and the integration of generative AI with predictive scoring systems. This review identifies a prioritised agenda for future empirical research in predictive B2B marketing intelligence and establishes a consolidated conceptual foundation.
&lt;/div&gt;
