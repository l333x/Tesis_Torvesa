## Resumen (de la base de datos, no de la lectura)
This paper examines how AI-authored and human-authored first-touch B2B outreach emails differ in the signals they send and how those differences are associated with early-stage engagement outcomes. The focus is on first-touch outreach because an unfamiliar business sender contacts a potential customer before trust exists, so the message itself becomes the basis for judgment. Using ten campaigns and five interviews, this study compares four signal dimensions: credibility, relevance, authenticity or human signal, and automation-like signal. The findings show that AI-authored emails were often stronger on structured credibility, while human-authored emails more often showed stronger relevance, stronger authenticity, and more visible contextual adjustment. The main point is that, in first-touch outreach, polish alone is not enough. The message must also make the sender appear legitimate, relevant, and worth engaging.
