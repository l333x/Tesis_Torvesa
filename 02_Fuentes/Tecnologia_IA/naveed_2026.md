## Resumen (de la base de datos, no de la lectura)
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets language models answer questions from an organization's own documents, with citations, and without training on proprietary content. Prototypes are easy to build, but a production enterprise system inherits the weaknesses of its repositories, including stale and duplicate content and inconsistent permissions, and introduces new risks: a user may receive information they were never entitled to read, or a confident answer drawn from a superseded policy. This paper proposes SE-RAG, a secure enterprise RAG architecture covering ingestion, parsing, chunking, embedding, hybrid retrieval, reranking and grounded generation, with identity, authorisation, document-level security and audit designed as properties of every stage. It presents a taxonomy of leakage paths and matching controls, a pre-filtering approach to permission enforcement, a layered strategy for reducing hallucination and verifying grounding, and a governance model that assigns ownership of content, access, models and operations. An evaluation design specifies retrieval, generation and operational metrics together with security test cases, and the architecture is analysed across five enterprise scenarios. The paper argues that retrieval quality, content governance and permission design determine the success of enterprise RAG more than the choice of generative model. Experimental validation is left for future work.
