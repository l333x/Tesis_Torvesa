## Resumen (de la base de datos, no de la lectura)
The increasing use of personal data in corporate activities has raised complex legal questions concerning the distinction between data protection violations and criminal offences attributable to corporations. This study examines the construction of corporate criminal liability under Law Number 27 of 2022 concerning Personal Data Protection, particularly the relationship between prohibited conduct, criminal fault, and corporate attribution. Employing normative juridical research through statutory and conceptual approaches, the study analyses the Personal Data Protection Law alongside Indonesia's corporate criminal liability framework. The findings establish a five-stage analytical framework: identifying the applicable statutory offence; establishing its constituent elements and required fault; determining the perpetrator's organisational role; examining the connection between the conduct and corporate activities; and assessing corporate attribution and sanctions. This framework distinguishes administrative non-compliance, individual criminal misconduct, and corporate criminal liability. It further demonstrates that corporate benefit or an employment relationship alone does not establish criminal responsibility, while negligent security failures cannot automatically satisfy offences requiring intentional conduct. The study also identifies the limitations of existing criminal provisions in addressing serious corporate negligence. Accordingly, a narrowly defined strict liability mechanism is discussed exclusively as a de lege ferenda proposal, subject to statutory safeguards. The proposed framework contributes to a more systematic and legally certain assessment of corporate criminal liability in personal data protection cases.
