## Resumen (de la base de datos, no de la lectura)
The constant dynamics of adaptation and change required by a globalized world entail demands that directly impact teaching–learning processes for the development of a global citizen. Consequently, in higher education, institutions face the challenge of establishing strategic academic planning to transform teaching–learning models across all instructional modalities. This highlights the need for academic administration to devise and implement diverse strategies to develop experiential learning at the program and course levels, according to the instructional format. This article presents an analysis of the implementation of strategic design in the planning of experiential learning through a design thinking approach. The goal is to operationalize a more meaningful, lasting, and competency-aligned education that is responsive to the development of global citizenship. Accordingly, a review of the higher education literature integrates international frameworks—such as UNESCO’s pillars of education and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)—with student-centered adaptive planning models. The paper introduces and analyzes the Experiential Planning Model through Design Thinking, developed by the author, as a tool for curricular innovation. The model proposes a structured approach from empathy to evaluation, integrating techniques for the inclusion of experiential learning strategies grounded in principles of strategic thinking. The proposal is supported by international research and by authors who conceive the educational process as an intentional design practice aimed at fostering deeper and more enduring learning. Additionally, the structure of the model for transitioning from presencial to virtual instruction using design thinking (Díaz Muñoz, 2022) served as a conceptual reference for defining the proposed model. The article concludes with recommendations for educators, curriculum designers, and higher education institutions seeking to transition toward transformative pedagogical models. Likewise, directions for future research are articulated, focusing on the validation of the proposed model, the impact of strategic design on the development of global competencies, and the role of design thinking as a driver of pedagogical innovation.
