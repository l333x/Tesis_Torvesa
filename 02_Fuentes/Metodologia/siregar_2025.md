## Resumen (de la base de datos, no de la lectura)
Mixed methods research emerged as an effort to integrate the two dominant research paradigms—quantitative and qualitative—to broaden the understanding of a phenomenon by incorporating multiple perspectives, assumptions, data collection techniques, and analytical strategies aligned with the research objectives. This study aims to critically examine the concept, characteristics, and application of mixed methods research within the broader methodological landscape of scholarly inquiry. A library research (literature-based) approach was employed, relying on the analysis of current and relevant academic literature. The findings indicate that mixed methods research not only combines two distinct methodological traditions but also serves as a strategic approach to address the limitations or weaknesses inherent in using either approach in isolation. The integration of quantitative and qualitative data enables researchers to generate findings that are more comprehensive, valid, and contextually grounded. Mixed methods research is rooted in the pragmatist paradigm, which prioritizes practical application and problem-solving over strict adherence to any single methodological doctrine. Consequently, this approach offers both flexibility and depth in addressing complex research questions.
