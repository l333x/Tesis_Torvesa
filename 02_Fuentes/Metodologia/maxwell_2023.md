## Resumen (de la base de datos, no de la lectura)
This chapter presents a particular understanding of realism, one that has been promoted in both philosophy and social research methodology, as a useful conceptual tool for mixed methods researchers. This understanding of realism combines ontological realism (there is a real world that exists independent of our perceptions and theories) with epistemological constructivism (our understanding of reality is inevitably our own construction, rather than a direct and infallible perception). This conception is particularly useful in designing and conducting mixed methods social research, in five main ways. First, it views meanings and other mental phenomena as real, although these are understood using a different conceptual language (the mental framework) from physical phenomena. Second, it includes culture as part of the mental framework, as the system of beliefs, values, goals, and so on held by members of a social group. Third, it treats individual and group diversity, cultural as well as social, as a real and important phenomenon, rather than something that can be ignored or dismissed in focusing on averages or numerically dominant features of groups or settings. Fourth, it accepts causation as a legitimate concept in both qualitative and quantitative research, for both the physical and mental frameworks, and sees the two categories of phenomena as causally interacting in shaping behaviour. Fifth, it conceptualises research designs not only as constructions of the researcher but also as real properties of the research ‘on the ground’, ones which may differ from the researcher’s conceptions. These perspectives give realism a significant value for planning and conducting mixed methods research in the social sciences.
